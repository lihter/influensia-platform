Client edit rules
Full edit until Spec frozen. After that a change request the agent accepts or rejects (it may reopen the RFQ). After Order confirmed only agents change quantity, spec or packaging, and the platform records the cost and time impact. Cancellation is free until Order confirmed, minus sample costs incurred.
Multi-line reality
A request has lines; lines can end up with different suppliers, lead times and outcomes. Sourcing status is per line. Execution groups lines into POs per supplier and shipments; the client sees a roll-up per line ("3 of 5 lines in production").
Repeat and cross-sell orders
A repeat order references the same Product and spec version and skips the sourcing lane unless the agent flags a re-quote. Items picked from a factory range create request lines pre-linked to that supplier: no RFQ, straight to Negotiating → Offer ready.

Enforcement lives in the data layer

  • Supplier tables sit in a separate schema; the client-facing API has no join path to them.
  • Every client-visible text or file passes a leak check before publish: supplier names from the DB, Alibaba/1688 URLs, WeChat IDs — the same discipline as the OOXML grep on RFQ files, applied on the way out.
  • Images are agent-approved, EXIF-stripped and logo-cropped; supplier originals stay internal. Factory ranges are re-rendered by us, never the factory's own PDF.
  • Documents default to internal; an agent flips each one to client-visible. Types that inherently name the factory (factory PI, B/L, manufacturer-named test reports) are replaced in principal mode by our own PI/invoice and applicant-named reports.
  • Audit log of everything a client has been shown; disclosed_to on a supplier once any client has seen its name.

08 · Problems in the brief

Nine things that need a decision, not a feature

"Never disclose the factory" cannot be a UI rule

Why it matters
The factory's name sits on its PI, the B/L shipper field, the customs entry and most test reports — and, per Bogdan's flow, on the 30% deposit wire and the forwarder handover. Under GPSR (Reg. 2023/988) the manufacturer's name, postal and electronic address must be on the product or packaging, and a private-label brand owner is deemed the manufacturer — so unbranded goods carry the factory's name into the client's warehouse.
Recommendation
Anonymity is structurally possible only for private-label goods, principal-mode shipments via an agency-controlled export entity, our own forwarder, and reports issued to the applicant. Model it: commercial_mode per order, disclosed_to per supplier, and the catalog stays anonymous to everyone else. Decide whether Bogdan's or Cristina's China entity, or a paid export agent, is the shipper of record.

"Same price for everyone" is a fiction

Why it matters
A price is a function of spec version, quantity, incoterm, validity and FX date. Publishing "$0.054/pc" without those five attributes creates a promise the next quote cannot keep.
Recommendation
Catalog shows a reference price that carries all five. In v1 every catalog order passes an agent confirmation before it becomes an offer; auto-accept within pricing rules comes later, once there is data on how often the reference price holds.

"They should tell the truth" about repeat volume needs a mechanism

Why it matters
A forecast field costs the client nothing to inflate and wins them a lower price. Negotiating with factories on inflated volume burns credibility with the factory, not the client.
Recommendation
Structured forecast (first-order qty, reorder interval, 12-month volume). The offer shows two prices: trial and committed-volume. The committed price is a framework that reverts or trues up if the volume is not met. The platform tracks forecast vs actual per client and shows agents a forecast-accuracy score before the next negotiation.

Requests are multi-line and orders split across suppliers

Why it matters
A single header status is wrong from the first real order: five sleeve SKUs from one factory, toploaders from another, albums still unquoted.
Recommendation
Status per line in sourcing, per PO / shipment in execution, roll-up for the client. This is the single most important schema decision.

"Agent decides per product" still needs a T&C default

Why it matters
Without a written default, the first client who finds their product in the catalog has a dispute with no anchor — and clients selling on the same marketplace will find it.
Recommendation
Client agreement states: "we may offer sourced products to other clients unless exclusivity is agreed in writing", with an optional paid exclusivity window. The per-product decision then operates inside a rule.

The KOFF chain

Why it matters
Order 312 came from a KOFF customer through KOFF. Either KOFF is a partner tier that sees its sub-clients' orders, or its customers become direct clients — and Bogdan's employment conflict applies either way.
Recommendation
Decide the tier before the schema (Q A2). A partner tier is one extra table and a visibility rule now; retrofitting it later means migrating every order's ownership.

Adoption

Why it matters
CRMs die when agents stop updating them; then the client portal shows stale milestones and the WhatsApp questions come back.
Recommendation
Status changes as by-products of work (see the state table), stale-state alerts after five working days, nine client milestones rather than twenty-four, and one named owner of status freshness (Q G3).

Time budget

Why it matters
The platform earns nothing itself; only client orders do. A custom build is realistic at 4–6 weeks solo for v1 including migration from the tracker sheets — and v0.2 added a supplier funnel, cost models, payment milestones and factory ranges to the scope.
Recommendation
Time-box to 4 weeks, internal-first; factory ranges and proposals are v1.1. Kill condition: fewer than three paying clients with active orders at go-live means Sheets plus a thin portal would have been enough. Validation test for the catalog hypothesis: send a PDF price list of sourced products to current clients before writing any marketplace code.

Two payment vocabularies

Why it matters
Bogdan's flow says "send 30% + commission"; Marko decided "one all-in price, margin hidden". Both are legitimate, but they are different products: a visible commission on top of a factory price is agency mode; a single price is principal mode. A client shown both wordings will ask what the commission is on the all-in price.
Recommendation
Per client, one vocabulary, driven by commercial_mode: principal clients see "30% deposit / 70% on QC pass" against our invoice; agency clients see "30% deposit to the factory + our fee" and know the factory. Decide which one Piotr's team is on before the first offer goes out (Q M1).
